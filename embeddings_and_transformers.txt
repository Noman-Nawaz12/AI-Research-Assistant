Sentence Transformers and Embedding Models
============================================

What Are Embeddings?

An embedding is a numerical representation of text (a word, sentence, or paragraph) in the form of a
dense vector, typically containing anywhere from 100 to 1500 numbers. These vectors are designed so
that texts with similar meaning are placed close together in the vector space, while unrelated texts
are placed far apart. This allows machines to compare meaning mathematically rather than relying on
exact word matches.

The all-MiniLM-L6-v2 Model

all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is a lightweight sentence embedding model built on the MiniLM architecture and fine-tuned
using the Sentence Transformers framework. It converts a sentence or paragraph into a 384-dimensional
vector. Despite its small size, it achieves strong performance on semantic similarity tasks while being
significantly faster than larger transformer models like BERT-large. This makes it a popular choice for
retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) applications where speed and efficiency matter.

How Embeddings Enable Semantic Search

Traditional keyword search looks for exact matches between the search query and stored documents.
Semantic search, powered by embeddings, instead compares the meaning of the query to the meaning of
stored text chunks. For example, a query like "How do I reset my password?" can successfully retrieve
a document chunk that says "Steps to recover account access" even though the words are different,
because their embeddings are close together in vector space.

Cosine Similarity

Cosine similarity is the most common metric used to compare two embedding vectors. It measures the
angle between two vectors rather than their magnitude, producing a score between -1 and 1, where 1
means the vectors point in exactly the same direction (identical meaning) and 0 means they are
unrelated. Many vector databases, including FAISS, can be configured to use cosine similarity by
normalizing vectors before indexing and using inner product search.

Why Model Choice Matters in RAG Pipelines

Choosing the right embedding model affects both retrieval quality and system performance. Larger models
may capture more nuanced meaning but are slower and require more memory, while smaller models like
all-MiniLM-L6-v2 offer a good balance of speed and accuracy for most document question-answering use
cases. In production systems, developers often benchmark multiple embedding models against their own
dataset before choosing one for deployment.
